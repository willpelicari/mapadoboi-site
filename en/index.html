What's inside

Cuts on the steer

The cuts are drawn on a Nelore or Angus steer, your choice, and tapping a piece shows where it sits on the animal, how tender and fatty it is, and the other names it goes by.

One cut, many countries

A love of barbecue shouldn't stop at a language border: the piece Brazilians call picanha is top sirloin cap in the United States and tapa de cuadril in Argentina. Since every country splits the steer its own way, the app shows the equivalents between the charts, so you know what to ask your butcher when a recipe comes from abroad or when you travel.

  • Brazil, with the Gaúcho variant

Find the right cut

Pick the tenderness, the amount of fat and the cooking method, and the map highlights only the pieces that suit what you plan to make.

Scan the label

Take a photo of the butcher or supermarket label, and the app identifies the cut with a reading done on your device, without sending any image to the internet.

Step-by-step recipes

From the grill to the oven, every recipe is tied to the right cut and gives the time, temperature and doneness for each step.

Cooking on the Lock Screen

Once you start a recipe, each step's time shows on the Lock Screen, so you can follow along without opening the app.

Search by any name

Ribeye, bife ancho, entrecôte or contrafilé: type the name you know, whether it is the official one, the one used at the market or a regional one, in any language, and the app finds the right cut.

Every cut

Each cut has its own page, showing where it sits on the animal, what other countries call it and how to cook it.

Where the data comes from

Mapa do Boi’s charts and grades follow official standards and published research, which are also cited inside the app.

Brazil
Ordinance No. 5/1988 of the Ministry of Agriculture (MAPA), with market names from ABIEC and, in the Gaúcho variant, the names used in Rio Grande do Sul
Argentina
IPCVA and SENASA beef cuts nomenclature
United States
USDA IMPS specifications and the NAMP Meat Buyer’s Guide, with retail names from the Beef Checkoff
Canada
CFIA Meat Cuts Manual, with retail names from Canada Beef and Quebec terms from the OQLF
Portugal
Standards NP 1989:2006 and NP 1990:2006 from the Portuguese Quality Institute (IPQ)
Tenderness
Studies from the University of Nebraska, Texas A&M and the USDA published in journals such as Meat Science and the Journal of Animal Science, plus Brazilian research on Nelore cattle
Fat
Brazil’s food composition table (TACO), from Unicamp, and the USDA nutrient database
