Filé de costela (no. 8) · Brazilian

A thick prime rib steak with the whole rib bone left on and scraped clean, about 30 cm long. The meat is the same as a ribeye, marbled and juicy. Its thickness calls for heat control: a hard sear, then indirect heat.

In other countries

  • Argentina: Bife ancho
    The filé de costela is the rib eye: the longissimus over the 6th to 10th thoracic vertebrae. The bife ancho is that eye with its cap, which Brazil sells apart as the capa de filé, so it holds more. A bife ancho sin tapa is the closest match.
  • United States: Ribeye
    The ribeye is the same rib eye: the longissimus with the spinalis and the small muscles beside it. The US rib runs from the 6th to the 12th rib, a little longer than the filé de costela's 6th to 10th vertebrae.
  • Portugal: Acém redondo
    The acém redondo is the back of the Portuguese acém, over the 6th to 10th ribs: the longissimus with the muscles that cover it. Brazil sells those two layers apart, the filé de costela (the eye) and the capa de filé (the trapezius and latissimus over it), so each is one part of the acém redondo.

How to cook it

Two-zone grilled tomahawk

BBQ · 35 min · Direct sear, then indirect heat

Done when: Pull at 54 °C center

  1. Pat the meat dry and wrap the bare bone in foil.
  2. Season the whole 5 cm steak generously with coarse salt.
  3. Sear over strong embers for 4 minutes per side, then move to the cool side for 25 minutes.
  4. Pull at 54 °C and rest 7 minutes before carving the meat off the bone.

Reverse sear

Oven · 60 min · Oven at 110 °C, then very hot embers

Done when: Pull at 54 °C center

  1. Season the tomahawk with crushed coarse salt and black pepper.
  2. Roast at 110 °C for about 50 minutes, to 45 °C in the thickest part.
  3. Move to very hot embers for 2 to 3 minutes per side to build a crust.
  4. Pull at 54 °C, slice, and serve right away.

Straight on the coals

BBQ · 25 min · Directly on glowing coals, no ash

Done when: Pull at 54 °C center

  1. Fan the charcoal to blow off all loose ash.
  2. Press coarse salt onto the well-dried meat.
  3. Lay the tomahawk right on the coals for 5 minutes per side, turning once.
  4. Pull at 54 °C, brush off any ash, tent with foil, and rest 8 minutes.
